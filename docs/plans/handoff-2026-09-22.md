## 2 — The finding that should drive everything next

Three gameweeks of decomposition, plus a split that only became visible at GW5.

**S1–S5 has never moved the metric**: +0.003, −0.006, +0.0003.
**S6(a) is large and unstable**: +0.029, +0.0065, −0.033 — mean near zero, spread
wider than every margin any decision here has turned on.

**And the two metrics disagree about where we are weak:**

| | `spearman_played` | `spearman_all` |
|---|---|---|
| GW3 | component **wins** | tie |
| GW4 | component loses | component **loses** |
| GW5 | component **wins** | component **loses** |

`spearman_all` is effectively the *"will he play"* problem — most of 659 players
score zero. We lose it to `naive/1.0` and to `official-ep/1.0`. We win
`spearman_played`, the *"how many points given he played"* problem.

**So the rate half of the model earns its place and the minutes half is worse than
doing almost nothing — and `component/1.2` spent its entire effort on the minutes
half.** That is why it did not pay, and it is the single most useful thing this
project has learned.

## 3 — Model 1.3, the agreed direction

Two changes only, **so that attribution survives**:

1. **Subtract.** Revert the minutes elaboration to the simplest availability term
   that works. At GW5 `component/1.1-price` (0.41423) and
   `component/1.2-availability` (0.41450) are indistinguishable, and both beat
   `component/1.2` (0.38173). The S1–S6 apparatus is not paying for itself.
2. **Replace the fixture-strength model.** `attack_scale = rates.xg_for /
   league_mean_goals`, and `xg_for` currently comes from `DifficultyBandRatesModel`
   — the FPL difficulty band, a 1–5 integer lookup. Replace with a fitted team
   attack/defence model (Poisson, shrunk toward the league mean). **The seam already
   exists**: `strength.py` defines a `TeamRatesModel` Protocol with `rates()` and
   `name()`, designed for exactly this (projections-v1.md Decision 1).

**Register three variants, not one, and register them all up front.** Two changes in
one variant cannot be attributed, which is the same problem the two-change 1.2
bundle had. FU-60 §6.1 recorded that registering the decomposition variant *late*
was a mistake because it could never be scored on GW3. Do not repeat it:

- `component/1.3-fixtures` — new strength model, minutes term **unchanged**
- `component/1.3` — new strength model **and** the simplified minutes term
- `component/1.1-price` and `component/1.2` stay registered as the controls

Then `1.3 − 1.3-fixtures` isolates the minutes change and `1.3-fixtures −
1.1-price` isolates the fixture change, exactly as S6(a)/S1–S5 were separated.

**Deliberately not in 1.3** — the other ideas are parked so that 1.3 stays
attributable: blending `ep_next` (parsed and currently ignored; `official-ep/1.0`
tops `spearman_all`), rank-ensembling the models already scored each run, and
splitting P(plays) from E[points | plays] as two separately evaluated problems.

**Not FU-62.** Spearman is invariant to monotone transforms, so the compression
into [0, 5.2] **cannot** hurt either ranking metric. It hurts magnitudes, which only
the optimiser consumes — so it sequences behind FU-72, not in front of a model
change.

**Not FU-57 or FU-58.** Their premise is gone; both entries now say so.

## 4 — The backtest asset, and its exact limit

**Four gameweeks are backtestable, not five.** GW2, GW3, GW4 and GW5 each have a
complete pinned trio in `~/fpl-pinned/` — pre-deadline capture, fixtures, settled
live payload.

**GW1 is not available and cannot be made available.** Its deadline was
2026-08-21T17:30Z; the capture archive begins 2026-08-28. There is no leak-free
pre-deadline snapshot, and per the standing finding that route is closed
permanently — no past gameweek's availability state is recoverable from the vaastav
archive, in any season.

**The line that keeps a backtest honest.** Fitting team ratings from *match results*
(goals for and against) is fitting to data and is fine. Tuning any constant against
*our evaluation metric* on four gameweeks is what FU-50 §3 forbids, and it is how
this would fool us. The distinction is what makes change 2 safe and it must be
written into the pre-registration, not assumed.

**GW2–GW5 are already contaminated as a clean test set** — every one of them has
been read in detail, and 1.3's design is a response to what they showed. That is
what a development set is *for*; it means GW6 must stay untouched until the
criterion is written.
